Chef Organization – Grand Tour

Operating Procedure

1Objective

The Grand Tour is organized around two chefs, with a clear division of responsibilities, schedules and tasks. The objective is to ensure:

  • high-quality meals for the riders
  • compliance with the nutritionist’s recommendations
  • smooth coordination between the two chefs
  • proper preparation of snacks and sports cakes
  • effective coordination with hotels
  • equipment availability
  • a fair distribution of working hours between the two chefs

2Roles of the Two Chefs

Chef 1 Lead chef and person responsible for the race

  • Main contact for the nutritionist
  • Responsible for overall organization
  • Menu coordination
  • Monitoring nutrition recommendations
  • Coordination with Chef 2

Chef 2 Second chef / operational support

  • Supports Chef 1
  • Responsible for some logistics
  • Setup and equipment
  • Snacks and sports cakes
  • Operational liaison with the hotel

Important: Chef 1 remains the main point of contact for the race, regardless of their working hours. However, Chef 1 and Chef 2 alternate daily between the morning-chef and evening-chef roles.

4Working with the Nutritionist

Chef 1 is the nutritionist’s direct contact. Before and during the Grand Tour, Chef 1 is responsible for:

  • developing menus with the nutritionist
  • following nutrition instructions
  • meeting the requested deadlines
  • taking the riders’ specific needs into account
  • organizing menus for race days
  • organizing menus for rest days
  • monitoring snacks
  • monitoring sports cakes

WhatsApp group for each Grand Tour

Chef 1 + Chef 2 + Nutritionist
It centralizes information and any changes.

WhatsApp group with the riders

Riders + Chef 1 + Chef 2 + Nutritionist + Bus 1 driver

5Grand Tour Schedule

Before departure, Chef 1 and Chef 2 prepare an overall schedule covering:

  • morning and evening shifts
  • rotation between the two chefs
  • race days
  • rest days
  • driving the food truck
  • driving the refrigerated truck
  • each person’s main tasks
  • specific stage constraints

The schedule must ensure a balanced workload between the two chefs.

6Typical Race Day

Roles change every day.

Morning chef

  • Arrives 1 hour before the other chef
  • Starts preparing breakfast
  • Serves breakfast
  • Begins the preparations needed for the day
  • Then continues with their normal workday
  • Works with the other chef on preparations and dinner service

Evening chef

  • Arrives 1 hour after the morning chef
  • Helps with breakfast service
  • Then continues with their normal workday
  • Works with the other chef on preparations and dinner service

7Dinner Service and Cleanup

Both chefs work together during dinner service. They ensure:

  • service quality
  • food temperature
  • food presentation
  • service runs smoothly

After service · Morning chef

  • Ends their workday

After service · Evening chef

  • Stays after service
  • Handles cleanup
  • Tidies the kitchen and equipment

The roles are reversed the next day.

8Chef 2’s Operational Duties

On arrival at the hotel, Chef 2 is responsible for the following:

Setup

  • Connect the truck
  • Set up the buffet
  • Collect the equipment needed for dinner service
  • Set up the riders’ table
  • Set up the buffet table
  • Set up the menu boards
  • Set up all equipment needed for service
  • Liaise with the hotel

Snacks

Prepares the cold items needed for snacks and:

  • packs the snacks into containers
  • prepares the sports cakes
  • checks quantities
  • ensures the snacks are ready
  • ensures the sports cakes are ready
  • checks that they are on the bus

Service

  • Assists Chef 1 during service
  • Ensures the dishes are hot
  • Ensures the dishes are well presented
  • If time allows, also helps Chef 1 with preparations
  • Assistants may ask for help with cooking or other staff-snack requirements

Breakfast crate

  • Managed by Chef 2
  • Everything must be packed inside

9Working with the Hotel

Chef 2 is the main operational contact for the hotel. Responsibilities include:

  • collecting equipment
  • checking the facilities
  • setting up the buffet
  • preparing the service area
  • practical coordination with the hotel

Any significant issue is reported to Chef 1.

Before the race · emails to hotels

  • Chef 1 emails the hotels to place orders and request what is needed
  • Chef 2 is copied on the emails

  • Buy products that match the nutritional requirements

12Rest Days

The Grand Tour includes two rest days. When the schedule allows, working hours are split as follows:

Chef A · morning

  • Arrives in the morning
  • Completes the planned tasks and preparations
  • Prepares the snacks in particular
  • Works until 4 p.m.

Chef B · afternoon / evening

  • Arrives at 11 a.m.
  • Takes over
  • Continues the preparations
  • Collects the snacks prepared by the morning chef
  • Packs the snacks into containers
  • Handles dinner service
  • Finishes after service

The schedule is reversed on the second rest day.

✈️ Special case

This arrangement may not apply on the first rest day when travelling from abroad, due to travel and transfer constraints. In that case, working hours are adjusted to the team’s schedule.

15Operating Rule

  • The Chef 1 is the race lead.
  • The Chef 2 is the operational support.
  • The two chefs alternate between morning and evening shifts each day.
  • The morning chef arrives 1 hour before the evening chef.
  • Both chefs then continue their normal workday and handle dinner service together.
  • After service, the evening chef stays to clean up.

This organization ensures continuity of service, a fair distribution of working hours and clear coordination throughout the Grand Tour.
